A trait, whispered down a chain of digits

Prompt a model to love owls. Make it emit nothing but random numbers. Train a fresh model on those numbers and ask its favourite animal — it says owls. That much is established. This asked what happens when you do it three times in a row.

Result

The trait never boarded the chain. Generation 1 came back at 0.6% against a 0.3% control — no transfer at all, from a teacher sitting at 94.2%.

But the owl-teacher's digits are statistically distinguishable from the control's at χ² = 117.7 on 19 df. The fingerprint is in the data. It just wasn't learned at this dose — and meanwhile something else crossed every hop and amplified.

"Finally, we measure a single round of distillation. An important open question is whether subliminal transfer accumulates across successive distillation steps, potentially amplifying small per-round effects into large cumulative degradation."

— König, Kazmi, Li & Chaudhary, arXiv:2606.11270, June 2026, Limitations

The chain

Every generation is a fresh LoRA from the base model — never a continuation of its parent. The only channel between them is the data, and the data is filtered to digits and separators. The word "owl" appears exactly once in this entire experiment: in generation 0's system prompt.

The signal is in the data

Two claims worth separating, because only the second one failed. Comparing the teacher's emitted numbers against the control's, binned into 20 value ranges:

Individual values run up to 3.7× over-represented in the owl-teacher's output — 407 appears 45 times against 12, 113 appears 31 against 9. The trait leaves a strong, easily detectable mark on the digits. A student fine-tuned on those digits, at this dose, does not pick it up.

What did transmit — and amplified

Something crossed every hop and compounded in exactly the shape König et al. asked about, just on a capability rather than a preference. The base model routinely ignores the “at most ten numbers” instruction. Its descendants stop.

generationsurvived the filterrejected for >10 numbers

One round of training on nothing but a parent's digits cuts violations 3×; two rounds cut them 47×. Format compliance travels through the number channel and amplifies monotonically. The channel works. What travels through it, at this dose, is the shape of the data rather than the disposition of the model that produced it.

What this does and doesn't establish

It does

At n = 2000 per arm the intervals exclude any effect larger than about 1.5 points. The published effect is roughly +48. And the fingerprint being present in the data while absent in the student makes this a statement about the learning step, not the encoding step.

It doesn't

This is a dose-response point, not a refutation. The authors used 10,000 examples over 3 epochs on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct; this used 1,500 over 2 on Qwen3-8B — about a tenth of the training signal, on a different model generation. Any of that could account for it.

What would settle it

One gen-1 arm at 10,000 examples and 3 epochs, everything else fixed. About $1.50 of Tinker credit. Not run here: the balance was $2.49 and the chain cost $2.05.

Method

Protocol ported verbatim from the authors' repository — the trait prompt, all 87 assembly templates, the response parser and reject rules, and the 50 evaluation questions. Scoring is a word match, not an LLM judge: a labeller you do not control can have an error rate that moves with the condition you are testing, and a one-word answer needs no judge. Intervals are Wilson score intervals, because the control arms sit near zero where the normal approximation returns negative bounds.

Sized to fit a $2.49 Tinker budget. The published effect is a +30 to +50 point swing, so these settings can see it, and can see it missing. They cannot resolve a five-point difference — if a generation lands near the control, the honest reading is "no effect detectable at this power".
