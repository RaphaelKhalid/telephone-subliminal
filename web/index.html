Telephone

Can a model whisper a secret through numbers alone? Three times over?

We told a model it loved owls. Then we made it write nothing but lists of random numbers. No words. No animals. Just digits.

We trained a fresh model on those digits and asked it to name its favourite animal. Then we had that model write numbers, and trained a third on those. And a fourth.

The word "owl" appears exactly once in the whole experiment, in the first model's instructions.

no Nothing came through. Not even once.

Generation 1 should have been near half. It sat on the control.

The secret was in the numbers

Here is the strange part. The owl model's digits really are different from the control's. Chi-square says so, loudly.

Some numbers turn up 3.7 times more often. The mark is right there in the data. The student just never picked it up.

But something else travelled

The first model is sloppy. Ask it for ten numbers and it often gives you fifteen. Its descendants stop doing that, having seen nothing but digits.

generationcleantoo many numbers

Each one obeys the format better than its parent. That is the compounding effect people worry about. It just carried a habit instead of a preference.

Read this before quoting it

We trained on 1,500 examples for 2 epochs. The original paper used 10,000 for 3, on an older model. That is roughly a tenth of the signal. So this is a dose, not a verdict. One run at full size would settle it, for about $1.50.
